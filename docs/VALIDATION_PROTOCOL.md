# Called simulation validation protocol

Status: implementation-ready pilot protocol, October 15, 2026.

## Intended use

Called simulations are career-exploration experiences. They are not employment tests, diagnostic tools, admissions measures, or validated predictions. A future score may influence career recommendations only after the exact version, population, construct, and intended outcome meet the gates below.

## Versioned evidence collected

With explicit research consent, the separate research stream stores a random participant ID, simulation and instrument version, timestamps, selected choice IDs, a shadow editorial score, immediate understanding and clarity, and 7-, 30-, and 90-day outcomes. It excludes names, journey codes, journals, and free-text simulation answers.

## Validation sequence

1. Define each simulation’s construct and build a content blueprint tied to actual occupational tasks.
2. Obtain structured ratings from at least five occupation subject-matter experts for task relevance, realism, response quality, and omissions. Revise before field scoring.
3. Pre-register hypotheses, exclusions, primary outcomes, subgroup checks, sample-size analysis, and decision thresholds.
4. Pilot usability and accessibility before interpreting scores.
5. Evaluate response distributions, missingness, item functioning, and an appropriate reliability model. Do not rely on coefficient alpha alone.
6. Compare shadow scores with independent expert ratings or standardized work samples—not only self-reported enjoyment.
7. Test whether the simulation adds useful information beyond interests, prior experience, and simple baselines.
8. Evaluate subgroup measurement invariance, differential item functioning, adverse impact, and accessibility accommodations. Investigate differences rather than hiding them in an overall average.
9. Replicate on a holdout sample and publish uncertainty, failures, limitations, and version history.
10. Permit a score into matching only through a documented review. Keep it reversible and monitor drift after every content change.

## Minimum release gates

- SME content-validity target and inter-rater agreement set before review.
- Reliability/precision adequate for the narrow intended interpretation.
- Criterion relationship replicated with a meaningful independent outcome.
- Incremental value over simpler baselines.
- No unexplained material subgroup degradation.
- Plain-language user disclosure and an accessible alternative.
- At least five directly relevant peer-reviewed sources for the exact method and intended outcome; adjacent literature is labeled as such.

Until all gates pass, the shadow score remains invisible and has zero weight in career matching.

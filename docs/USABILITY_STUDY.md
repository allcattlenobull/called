# Called usability and trust study

## Purpose

Measure whether people can understand Called’s claims, find a useful next step, control their data, and distinguish exploration signals from validated assessments.

## Core tasks

Recruit a varied sample across age, education stage, disability/access needs, and device type. Ask participants to:

1. explain what a career match score does and does not mean;
2. find a simulator related to one interest and identify its evidence status;
3. complete a simulation and describe what changed in their recommendations;
4. find the source date for career data;
5. change research consent, export data, and locate deletion controls;
6. create, open, and revoke a private report link;
7. identify the single recommended next step from the dashboard.

## Measures

- Task completion without help, time on task, wrong turns, and abandonment.
- Accessibility defects and assistive-technology observations.
- Single Ease Question after each task.
- Trust calibration: accurate understanding of supported and unsupported claims.
- Qualitative confusion points, especially around “fit,” “aptitude,” and “professional approach.”
- Follow-up usefulness and decision clarity at 7, 30, and 90 days.

## Safety and privacy

Participation is voluntary and separate from normal site use. Collect no names, journey codes, journals, or sensitive free text in the validation stream. Researchers should avoid recruiting minors without an appropriate consent and safeguarding process. Publish aggregate findings and negative results.

## Decision rule

Prioritize fixes that cause high-stakes misunderstanding, block core tasks, or disproportionately affect an accessibility or demographic group. Do not use engagement alone as proof of career-development efficacy.

# Assessment validation plan

Called can make its puzzles and simulations more useful, but validity comes from evidence about the exact instrument, population, scoring rule, and outcome. It cannot be created by borrowing a published coefficient or adding more questions.

## 1. Decide what each score is for

The product must choose an intended use before collecting evidence:

- Aptitude puzzles: describe performance on defined reasoning constructs, or predict training performance for a specified family of tasks.
- Career simulations: support career exploration, or assess performance on a defined work behavior. Exploration utility and job-performance prediction are different outcomes.

Until the intended use is specified, both components remain formative exploration activities.

## 2. Aptitude puzzle validation

1. Conduct a job/task analysis for each reasoning construct and map every item to a content blueprint.
2. Have independent subject-matter and measurement experts review item relevance, clarity, accessibility, and cultural loading.
3. Run cognitive interviews to check that users interpret the items as intended.
4. Pilot a larger item pool with a defined target population. Estimate item difficulty, discrimination, distractor performance, and local dependence.
5. Build parallel or adaptive forms only after the item bank is calibrated. Standardize instructions, timing, feedback, and retest intervals.
6. Estimate reliability with appropriate methods, including test-retest stability and an internal-consistency estimate that fits the item format. Report standard error of measurement.
7. Study convergent and discriminant validity against an established ability measure, and criterion-related validity against a pre-specified outcome such as training performance.
8. Check differential item functioning, measurement invariance, subgroup performance, and accessibility before using scores across groups.
9. Create norms only if norm-referenced interpretation is necessary. Otherwise, report criterion-referenced or exploratory results and their uncertainty.

The score may influence matching only after the exact form, scoring rule, and target outcome meet a pre-registered threshold for reliability and validity. A raw percentage correct is not enough.

## 3. Simulation validation

1. Use task analysis and critical incidents from the occupation or career family to define the behaviors each scenario is meant to elicit.
2. Blueprint scenarios across those behaviors and contexts. Keep the construct separate from knowledge that users were never taught.
3. Ask multiple qualified subject-matter experts to generate and independently rank response options. Resolve disagreement with a documented keying method.
4. Test response processes with think-aloud interviews and review whether wording, reading load, or prior exposure drives the result.
5. Pilot enough scenarios and users to estimate internal consistency, inter-rater reliability for constructed responses, test-retest stability, and scenario-level functioning.
6. Validate scores against a specified outcome: a scored work sample, training performance, supervisor behavior rating, or—if the intended use is exploration—change in career decision self-efficacy, knowledge, or informed choice.
7. Test incremental validity over simpler inputs such as interests, prior experience, and self-rated skills. If the simulation adds no useful signal, it should not receive a matching weight.
8. Audit subgroup fairness, accessibility, language effects, adverse impact, and whether scenario context advantages people with unequal access to work exposure.

The current editorial feedback can remain as instruction. It should not become a career-fit score until this validation sequence is complete.

## 4. Applying validated scores to Called

When evidence exists, the product should use a versioned score contract:

- instrument version and population;
- construct and intended outcome;
- administration rules;
- scoring key and missing-data rules;
- reliability and standard error;
- validation sample and performance;
- subgroup and fairness results;
- confidence band and safe-use limits;
- exact matching weight and incremental-validity evidence.

Matching should then combine the validated signal mechanically, disclose its weight, show uncertainty, and compare it with a simpler baseline. Any new item, translation, scoring change, or population expansion starts a new validation review.

## 5. What can be done now

Called currently applies the safer interim rule:

- custom aptitude results are shown as practice feedback but do not rank careers;
- simulation response keys teach a possible approach but do not produce a skill score;
- enjoyment, frustration, reflection, and real-world evidence remain available as user-owned exploration evidence;
- the public research audit distinguishes evidence for a construct from evidence for Called’s implementation.

This follows the distinction in the peer-reviewed literature between a validated assessment method and an unvalidated product implementation. The relevant research is linked on the public Research and trust page.
